Analytics

Exploratory analysis of the seven inputs the model actually uses, for the current season.

Season

Which inputs separate a points finish from a non-score

How strongly each input tracks with scoring, from −1 (opposite) to +1 (matching). The colour says which direction helps and is spelled out on every bar and in the legend below — you never have to infer it from the bar's position.

Points-finish rate across each feature's range

Entries split into equal-sized groups by each feature (for the one yes/no feature, into its two actual states), then the share of each group that scored. A feature that separates cleanly shows a steady climb or fall; a flat row means the model gets little from it this season.

Feature

What the grid actually looks like on each input

Raw shape of each feature this season, before it's linked to any outcome — a sanity check on the chart above it. A lopsided shape here (most of the grid bunched at one end) is exactly what makes a feature's correlation swing on a handful of drivers rather than the whole field.

Which team is actually delivering the points

Two different questions side by side: the bar is a team's strike rate — how often it scores when it has the chance. The donut is its share of every points finish handed out this season — who is actually banking the points, which a two-car team's rate alone won't tell you if a rival fields more cars.

Do rookies close the gap as the season goes on?

Cumulative points-finish rate through each round — rookies vs. everyone else. A single round only has 3 rookie entries, so a per-round rate would swing between 0% and 100% and say nothing; running the average keeps growing the sample as the season plays out.
